Design decisions

The first major consideration in this project was how to get data from the drone to the ground. I first thought of LTE, as I had used Websockets in previous projects, but LTE did not match the project specifications. A direct connection to our viewing laptop was wanted. As the main concern here was reliability, we settled on the Digi XBee SX 868. It occupied a legal European band, was extremely reliable, and was easy to interact with in code.


The next question was how the data we had should be visually displayed. I looked at a couple of options, such as a custom React display or graphing software. I decided on Grafana, as it is customizable, easy to use, and interacts with databases seamlessly.

Testing and results

I tested every aspect of this system, starting with datarate to the graphs, readings-to-display lag, database lag, and reliability with physical objects blocking at various distances. Due to European laws on RF bands, we could only have a reliable packet rate of 2 Hz, but it was extremely reliable. I found it had no problems behind smaller objects like trees up to 300m, and it worked well with line of sight past 1km, which was our specification.
