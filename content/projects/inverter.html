Design decisions

Our goal was to make a motor inverter from scratch for our Cascadia Motion CM200DX motor controller. The first important component we looked at was high-current power transfer. As this is a high-voltage motor controller, we need high voltage and current to flow through some of our circuits, so we had to take into account trace width, electrical characteristics of components, and heat transfer.


When creating our first half-bridge board, we chose high-power MOSFETs to control power, and due to current limits with our components, we used two MOSFETs for both high and low switching with heat sinking on the back.


For the control scheme, we had to link the high-power gate driver circuit to a Microcontroller, but didn't want our high power to leak into it if things went wrong. So we electrically isolated it using an optocoupler and separate ground, protecting its delicate circuitry.


As for the microcontroller, I was in charge of choosing it and writing code for it. I landed on the STM32G474 due to its reliable timers and PWM, multiple accurate ADCs, speed, deadtime capabilities, and general speed. It also has heritage as a motor controller used specifically for this purpose, and I had had STM32 experience, so it was the obvious choice. In order to ensure proper timing for tasks, we decided on FreeRTOS as a framework, with tasks for motor control, ADC reading, synthesis, and reading/writing CAN frames.
